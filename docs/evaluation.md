## 3. Dataset Provenance & Split Architecture

To evaluate the system without infringing on proprietary third-party capture sessions, evaluation was performed on a high-fidelity synthetic kinematic dataset:

### Data Partitioning

```
data/processed/pose/
├── X_train.npy   (8,000 sequences x 1,920 features)
├── y_train.npy   (8,000 ground truth labels)
├── X_test.npy    (2,000 sequences x 1,920 features)
└── y_test.npy    (2,000 ground truth labels)
```

- **Independent Test Partition:** Model evaluation (`src/models/evaluate.py`) executes solely on the held-out `X_test.npy` split.

## 5. Engineering Transparency: Real-World Generalization & Domain Gaps

In accordance with responsible machine learning reporting, perfect metrics on synthetic test distributions must be contextualized with real-world computer vision challenges:

1. **Camera Angle Variation:** The synthetic simulator models frontal and slightly oblique camera angles. Severe pitch ($> 45^\circ$) or overhead mounting degrades MediaPipe depth ($z$-coordinate) estimations.
2. **Clothing & Occlusion:** Loose clothing or desk obstructions can obscure hip and knee keypoints. The system mitigates this via the **Biomechanical Safety Guard** rule set, which defaults to upper-body spinal slope when lower joints have low confidence ($< 0.5$).
3. **Motion Blur:** High-speed running under low-light conditions causes optical shutter blur, reducing MediaPipe tracking confidence.
4. **Physical Kinematic Differences:** Real-world individuals exhibit heterogeneous body proportions, gait abnormalities, and spinal curvatures, necessitating threshold calibration for personalized posture alerts.
